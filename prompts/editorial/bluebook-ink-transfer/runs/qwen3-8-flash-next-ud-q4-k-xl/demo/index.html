Plate 01 / The Chemistry

The Chemistry of Reading

Cyanotype transfer · Iron(III) · Reaction time

Cyanotype is a photographic process in which the image is not captured by a camera but coaxed out of light by a solution of iron salts. Read slowly and the paper drinks the pigment evenly. Read fast and the ink smears across the grain. The folio you are holding is calibrated to your pace.

Between the emulsion and the fibre sits a narrow band of velocity — the perfect transfer zone. Inside it, the drawing resolves into sharp, high-contrast lines. Step outside it in either direction and the medium turns against you: too quick and the image drags; too still and it blooms.

The gauge in the lower right reads your scroll velocity in real time. It is not decoration. It is the readout of a process that is, for the duration of this page, genuinely running.

Marginal note — 01 Sir John Herschel, 1842. The first cyanotypes were botanical, not architectural. The medium was always about contact.
Plate 02 / The Mechanism

Velocity as a Variable

Scroll physics · Smudge · Bleed · Recovery

The reading of a building is a transfer of information from surface to memory. This folio treats that transfer as literal: your scroll rate drives a simulated chemical reaction, and the page rewards a steady hand.

When velocity exceeds the drag threshold, the emulsion is pulled sideways faster than it can set. The result is a horizontal smear — smudge. When it falls below the soak threshold, pigment creeps outward into the fibre and the edges soften into a halo — bleed.

Neither state is a failure. Both are recoverable. Ease back to the ideal band and the drawing snaps into focus, exactly as a plate lifted from the wash reveals a clean line.

You are not scrolling a document. You are holding a plate under running water. — On the mechanics of transfer

Plate 03 / The Structure

The Truss, Transferred

Steel · Warren configuration · 24 m span

A Warren truss distributes load along a lattice of equilateral triangles — the most economical form of carrying weight across a span. Drawn in iron salts, its members read as pure line: tension and compression reduced to a single stroke.

Watch the members resolve as you scroll. In the ideal band each node is a clean intersection. In smudge the diagonals run together into a grey field; in bleed the joints swell and merge.

Marginal note — 03 The diagonal carries both. Reversing the load reverses the member. Nothing here is fixed.
Plate 04 / The Facade

Reading a Facade

Section · Fenestration · Threshold

A facade is a boundary written twice — once in structure, once in light. The section below cuts through a glazed elevation: a rhythm of mullions, a bracketed canopy, and the quiet register of a floor line.

Read it at the pace the ink wants. Let the lines set. When the gauge holds in the ideal band, the whole elevation stands crisp against the blue, and the building is legible.

Marginal note — 04 A threshold is a decision the building makes on your behalf. Cross it slowly.
