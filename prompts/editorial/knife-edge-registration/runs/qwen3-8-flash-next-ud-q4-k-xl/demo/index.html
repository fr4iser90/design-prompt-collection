Ch. 01 — The Geometry of Sharpness

Sharpness is an angle, held.

A knife does not cut because it is thin. It cuts because two planes converge on a single line, and that line is kept true. Every edge is a promise of geometry — a contract between the steel and the stone.

Edge apex · 15–20° per side
Convergence tolerance · < 0.5 µm
Specimen 01

Bluntness is not wear. It is misalignment — the apex rolled, the bevels wandering off their intended plane. To restore an edge is to return it to its original line, degree by degree, stroke by stroke.

Roll resistance · high-carbon
Strop frequency · 12 passes / session

Ch. 02 — The Honing

Motion is the only honest tool.

The rod does not remove steel. It realigns it. Run the blade along the steel at a fixed angle, and the microscopic teeth — bent by use — return to their ranks. Speed is the enemy; patience is the abrasive.

Rod angle · 15°
Direction · heel → tip, alternating
Field Note

Watch the sparks. They are not the blade cutting — they are friction declaring its presence. Too fast, and you heat the temper, dulling the very edge you seek to save. Slow the hand. Let the steel remember its shape.

Friction heat limit · 150°C
Emitted light · 500–700 nm
04
Ch. 03 — The Stone

Water, grit, and silence.

The whetstone is a ledger. Each pass writes a line; each line is a degree. There is no shortcut through the grit — only the slow arithmetic of 1000, then 3000, then 8000, until the burr lifts like a whisper off the bevel.

Grit progression · 1k → 3k → 8k
Slurry · fresh, never recycled
Specimen 04

Steel is memory. A carbon blade forgives nothing and forgets nothing. Stainless lies, holding an edge that is not there. Choose the steel that tells the truth, then keep it honest with the stone.

Rockwell hardness · 60–64 HRC
Carbon content · 1.0–1.4%

Ch. 04 — The Test

Read the paper.

Hold a sheet at its corner. Bring the edge to it without pressure. If it parts cleanly, singing as it goes, the geometry holds. If it tears, the apex has rolled and the lesson begins again.

Test medium · bond paper, 80 gsm
Failure mode · tearing at apex
Maintenance

An edge is never finished. It is only maintained. Hone before every session, strop after every cut, stone when the paper finally resists. The blade is a discipline, not a possession.

Hone · daily · Strop · per use
Stone · when paper resists
Keep it sharp.
