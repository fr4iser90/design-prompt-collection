Ferrous Annual is a publication devoted to the afterlife of iron — to the slow chemistry that turns our most permanent material into powder, and to the histories that surface in the process. What follows is not a static record. Read it the way the metal itself is read: over time, the words corrode, and what lies beneath them begins to show.

I.On the Nature of Rust

Rust is not the end of iron; it is the iron becoming legible. For decades the plate holds its shape in silence, cold and grey and unbothered, until moisture finds a scratch and the reaction begins — iron, oxygen, water — a slow combustion that writes its own text across the surface.

We photograph these surfaces the way others photograph portraits. The flaking is the expression. The pitting is the punctuation. A bridge that has stood for a century speaks more clearly in its corrosion than in its commissioning plaque.

II.The Chemistry of Memory

Oxidation is a form of record-keeping. Every rainstorm, every salt-laden wind off the harbour, every night of condensation is logged in the thickness of the oxide layer. To read rust is to read a climate history compressed into colour.

The archive holds no dates because the dates are the colour itself. We index by hue: the bright orange of a fresh wound, the deep umber of a decade, the near-black of a century. Time, in iron, is a spectrum.

"Time is not the enemy of iron. Time is the author of its colour."

III.Field Notes from the Yard

The scrapyard at the edge of town is our most honest library. Here the machines are stacked like manuscripts, each one a draft of a century of labour. We catalog by decay rather than by make.

A turbine casing, split along a weld, reveals the grain of the metal like the rings of a tree. We press our thumbs into the flaking surface and feel the years come away in powder. Nothing here is permanent, and that is precisely why it endures.

IV.What the Rust Reveals

Polish a plate and you recover the surface; leave it and you recover the story. This publication is built on that tension — between the shine we preserve and the corrosion we let speak.

As you have read, the words have changed colour. That is the point. The archive does not pretend to be new. It shows you the layer beneath the ink, the history under the headline, the ghost of the machine that once stood where these letters now rest.
