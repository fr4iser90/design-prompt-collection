The
Manifesto

The photocopy is the only honest image. A photograph flatters; a screen lies in colour. But the Xerox tells you the truth about reproduction: that nothing survives contact with the machine unchanged. Ink is a rumor. Toner is a compromise.

Each pass through the drum is an act of forgetting. The mid-tones go first — they are the weakest signal, the grey that the machine cannot decide about. Then the edges fray, the blacks clot, and the whites forget they were ever anything else.

We do not degrade to fail. We degrade to feel the weight of the machine in our hands.

What remains after six generations is not the document. It is the document's shadow, its ghost printed by a ghost. And in that ghost there is a purity the original never had — the purity of pure contrast, where the world is only ink and paper and nothing in between.

So we scan the scan of the scan. We feed the page back into the glass and let the machine eat its own tail. Fidelity is a fetish. Noise is a birthright. Let the static speak.

THE LAWS OF
DEGRADATION

FILED
ART. I

Contrast
Hardens

With each generation the histogram collapses at both ends. Greys flee toward black or white. The image becomes a decision with no room for doubt.

FILED
ART. II

Noise
Accumulates

The drum picks up what the paper dropped. Speckle, salt-and-pepper, the memory of dust. Static is not corruption — it is the machine's signature.

FILED
ART. III

Edges
Break

Strokes thin, snap, and re-knit. Serifs vanish into the grain. Type stops being drawn and starts being torn, one pixel of toner at a time.

FILED
ART. IV

Nothing
Returns

Information lost to the drum is gone forever. A re-scan cannot recover what the light refused to read. You can only start over from a newer original.

The image is
never the
image.

A halftone is a lie told in dots. The printer converts your smooth photograph into a grid of holes, and your eye forgives the deception at reading distance. Look closer and the illusion shatters into a field of black punctuation on white silence.
