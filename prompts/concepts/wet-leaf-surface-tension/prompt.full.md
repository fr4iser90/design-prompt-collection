# Wet Leaf Surface Tension — Extended

**Concept:**
A study in stillness and physics. The night is quiet, and the only movement is the slow accumulation and release of dew. This is not about rain; it’s about condensation and surface tension. The visual appeal comes from the contrast between the matte/dark leaf and the high-gloss, lens-like water droplets.

**Palette:**
- **Leaf Base:** `#020202` (Near-black, but with subtle green undertones in highlights)
- **Leaf Highlight:** `#1a3b2a` (Deep emerald, visible only where light hits the waxy cuticle)
- **Moonlight:** `#e0f0ff` (Cool white, blue-shifted)
- **Shadow:** `#000000` (Pure black for depth)

**Type Pairing:**
- None. If caption is needed, use a refined serif (e.g., Playfair Display) in white, italic, small size, bottom-right, evoking botanical illustration labels.

**Layout:**
- Full-screen or large card.
- Centered composition. The leaf edge should be visible in one corner to provide context for the 'rolling off' interaction.

**Motion Brief:**
- **Entrance:** Droplets appear slowly, growing from tiny specks to full beads over 5-10 seconds.
- **Ambient:** Very subtle breathing of the leaf (slow scale pulse, <1%).
- **Interaction:**
  - **Hover:** Cursor acts as a 'wind' or 'disturbance'. Droplets near cursor move slightly towards each other (coalesce) if within a threshold.
  - **Click/Tap:** Causes a small ripple effect on the leaf surface, dislodging a droplet to roll off the edge.
  - **Physics:** Droplets must merge realistically (volume conservation, shape morphing) rather than just overlapping.

**Constraints Checklist:**
- [ ] No blue 'glow' effects; only reflections.
- [ ] Droplets must not look like glass beads; they must have refractive distortion.
- [ ] Leaf texture must be subtle, not noisy.
- [ ] Performance: Keep particle count low for high fidelity.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Droplets feel heavy and liquid.
- Lighting is crisp and cool, evoking night.
- Interaction feels delicate, not disruptive.
