# Desert Varnish Patina Map — Extended

## Concept
Desert varnish is a thin, dark coating that forms on rocks in arid environments over centuries. This concept uses that geological time-scale as a metaphor for data discovery. Information isn't just 'shown'; it is 'stained' onto the surface. The interface feels ancient, heavy, and immutable.

## Palette
- **Sandstone Base (`#D2B48C`):** The unvarnished rock.
- **Iron Oxide (`#8B5A2B`):** Early-stage varnish, rusty reds.
- **Manganese Rich (`#5C4033`):** Mature, dark varnish.
- **Cracked Light (`#F4E4BC`):** Highlights where the varnish has chipped or worn away.

## Typography
- **Display:** A serif typeface with irregular edges, mimicking hand-chiseled or wind-eroded lettering. Avoid clean digital serifs.
- **Body:** Small, all-caps monospace, embedded within the varnish pools.

## Layout
- **Asymmetric Grid:** Content blocks are irregular, fitting into the 'cracks' and 'crevices' of the rock texture.
- **Layering:** The varnish layer is above the text but semi-transparent. The text is only legible where the varnish is thick or where it has been 'polished' away by interaction.

## Motion Brief
1. **Ambient:** Slow, imperceptible darkening of the varnish layer. Dust settles on the surface.
2. **Interaction:** Hovering over a 'ridge' (high point of the texture) polishes the varnish away, revealing bright, clear text underneath. Moving away allows the varnish to slowly 're-stain' the area.
3. **Transition:** Data changes are shown by the varnish flowing like slow liquid into new shapes.

## Constraints
- Do not use smooth gradients. All transitions must have texture noise.
- The reveal must feel physical and chemical, not digital (no opacity fades).
- Colors must remain earthy; no synthetic blues or greens.

## Acceptance Criteria
- The varnish effect must react to the underlying topography (darker in low points).
- The 'polish' interaction must feel like rubbing stone.
