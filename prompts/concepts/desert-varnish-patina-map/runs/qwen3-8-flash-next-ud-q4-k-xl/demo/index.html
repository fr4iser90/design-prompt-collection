The coating itself

Desert varnish is a skin thinner than a fingernail — yet it records tens of thousands of years of arid exposure. Its darkness is chemistry, not pigment: manganese and iron oxides drawn up from dust and rainwater and cemented into the rock's surface.

Topography drives the stain

Why it pools

Water films run downhill and evaporate in hollows, so varnish accumulates thickest in crevices and low points while ridges stay pale and polished. The map obeys this rule exactly — the data is legible only where the surface geometry allows it, and interaction re-stains what the wind has rubbed clear.
