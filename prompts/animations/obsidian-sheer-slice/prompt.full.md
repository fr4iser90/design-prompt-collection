# Obsidian Sheer Slice — Extended

**Concept:**
A study of brittle fracture in volcanic glass. The animation captures the moment of cleavage in obsidian, emphasizing the conchoidal (shell-like) fracture patterns characteristic of amorphous silica. The aesthetic is cold, sharp, and high-contrast, playing on the duality of black glass and white light.

**Art Direction:**
- **Palette:** Dominant #050505 (void black), with #ffffff (pure white) speculars and subtle #3a1c1c (deep ember) internal glow.
- **Texture:** Smooth, reflective surface with micro-scratches. The fracture edges must look incredibly sharp and glassy.
- **Lighting:** High-contrast studio lighting. A single sharp directional light to define the form. No ambient fill to keep shadows deep and opaque.

**Motion Design:**
1.  **Rest State:** The obsidian block is stationary, catching a single streak of light.
2.  **Initiation:** A thin, bright line (stress) appears across the surface.
3.  **Fracture:** The crack propagates along curved, concentric arcs. The surface pieces separate slightly, moving with slow, heavy inertia.
4.  **Internal Reveal:** As the gap widens, a faint, cooling red glow emerges from the interior depth, suggesting recent cooling.
5.  **Settle:** The pieces come to rest, leaving a jagged, mirror-like valley.

**Technical Notes:**
- Use ray-marching or high-res normal maps to simulate the refractive index and reflection.
- Ensure the fracture edges are not blurred; they must be crisp.
- Avoid generic glass shattering; use conchoidal fracture logic.
