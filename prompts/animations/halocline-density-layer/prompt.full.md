# Halocline Density Layer — Extended

## Concept
This animation visualizes the concept of 'thresholds' and 'barriers' using the natural phenomenon of the halocline (where salinity changes sharply). It is suitable for a fintech or security brand that wants to convey 'robust defense' or 'clear distinction' without using aggressive shields or locks. The aesthetic is cool, sterile, and deeply aquatic.

## Palette
- **Fresh Water:** `#7fb6c9` (Turbid, particle-heavy)
- **Salt Water:** `#1a2a32` (Deep, clear, heavy)
- **Boundary Glow:** `#2d5c6b` (The mixing zone)
- **Signal:** `#e0f2f5` (Pure white/cyan light)

## Typography
- Minimal. If text is included, use 'Inter' or 'Roboto' (despite general rules, here we need clinical neutrality) or 'Söhne'. Keep it in the bottom right corner, tiny.

## Layout
- **Desktop:** Horizontal split. The boundary moves subtly up and down. 
- **Mobile:** Vertical flow. Data drops from the top of the screen into the deep.

## Motion Brief
1. **Fluid Sim:** Use a Navier-Stokes simulation or particle system to create the Kelvin-Helmholtz 'billows' where the two densities meet. This should be slow and hypnotic.
2. **Light Interaction:** Vertical beams of light represent data. When they hit the halocline, they do not pass through easily. They scatter and reflect, creating a 'glowing wall' effect.
3. **Depth:** Add a subtle 'rain' of particles in the top layer that slows down and stops when it hits the boundary, emphasizing the density change.

## Technical Constraints
- Avoid 'lava lamp' clichés. The mixing must be turbulent but controlled.
- The boundary should feel *heavy*.
- Use depth-of-field blur: the top layer is slightly out of focus, the boundary is sharp, the bottom is dark/void.

## Acceptance Criteria
- The distinction between the two layers is clear but organic.
- The interaction with light demonstrates 'barrier' behavior without explicit walls.
- The color palette stays within saline blues and teals.
