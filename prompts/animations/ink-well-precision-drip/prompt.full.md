# Ink Well Precision Drip — Extended

**Concept**
The moment of creation. Before a record exists, there is only the potential of the drop. This animation captures the precise, heavy nature of archival ink. It is a study in contrast: deep black fluid against sterile white, chaos (falling) resolving into order (text).

**Art Direction**
- **Palette:** Pure Black (#050505), Glossy Highlight (#1A1A1A), Paper White (#FAFAFA).
- **Texture:** The ink must look viscous, not watery. It should have a glossy, reflective surface that catches the north light.
- **Typography:** The revealed text 'INDEX' should be in a classic serif (e.g., Garamond or Baskerville), distorted by the fluid dynamics.

**Layout**
- **Desktop:** Split screen or centered focus. Pen enters from top-right.
- **Mobile:** Vertical composition. Pen from top, well at bottom.

**Motion Brief**
1. **Formation:** Ink beads at the pen nib. The bead grows, stretching downwards.
2. **Break:** The neck of the drop thins and snaps. The remaining ink on the pen recoils slightly.
3. **Fall:** The drop falls vertically. Highlight travels across its surface.
4. **Impact:** Contact with the well. The surface deforms. Ripples emanate from the center.
5. **Resolution:** The ripples dampen. The fluid settles. The text 'INDEX' becomes clear and legible within the ink's surface reflection.

**Constraints**
- No colorful inks. Strictly monochrome.
- The ripple effect must be physically plausible (damping over time).
- Text must remain legible despite distortion.

**Acceptance Criteria**
- The drop detachment must show realistic surface tension physics.
- The ripple should not look like a simple scale animation; it must displace pixels or vector points radially.
- The final state must be crisp and stable.
