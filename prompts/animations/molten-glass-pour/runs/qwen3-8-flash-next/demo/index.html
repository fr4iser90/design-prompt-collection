The Study ↓

The star of the show is not the glass — it is the light that passes through it.

Behaviour

The pour moves like honey, not water. A thick, continuous ribbon descends from the ladle, twisting gently on internal currents, its surface tension rounding each drip into a slow, heavy bead.

Every object behind the stream is distorted and magnified — the refractive index of 1.52 bends the backdrop exactly as real glass would, so the falling liquid reads as a lens rather than a fill.

There are no bubbles, no particles — pure liquid, lit only by its own heat.

The Cooling Gradient

Temperature is mapped to the vertical axis of the flow. At the mouth of the ladle the glass is pure white and emissive, throwing bloom into the dark. As it falls, heat bleeds into the air and the colour slides through amber and orange.

Near the pool it has lost its glow entirely — cooling to transparent, faintly cyan glass, where the only light is the refracted world passing through it.

The Pool

Where the ribbon meets the pool, ripples propagate slowly, damped by that same brutal viscosity. The pool glows faintly warm, reflecting the falling light back up the column — a closed loop of heat and reflection.

It is the contrast that sells it: hard finished glass against the fluidity of its origin.
