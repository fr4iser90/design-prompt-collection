# Glass Buoy Fresnel Focus — Extended

## Concept
This concept leverages the physics of refraction and the maritime heritage of the Fresnel lens to create a 'focus' metaphor. It is ideal for a search engine, insight platform, or luxury navigation brand. The beauty lies in the moment where chaos (blurred water) becomes order (sharp text) through the correct perspective.

## Palette
- **Deep Sea:** `#051f29` (Background base)
- **Mid Water:** `#144d5c` (Caustic highlights)
- **Glass Edge:** `#5ba4b5` (Specular highlights on glass)
- **Light:** `#ffffff` (Pure white light source)

## Typography
- The text is *formed* by light, not printed. It should look like a projection. Use a high-contrast serif or geometric sans-serif for the 'focused' text to maximize the refraction effect.

## Layout
- **Centered:** The lens is centered, filling 60% of the screen.
- **Background:** Full-bleed water texture.

## Motion Brief
1. **Rotation:** The lens rotates on its vertical axis. The rings are concentric, so the rotation creates a hypnotic, rippling visual.
2. **Refraction:** As the lens turns, the background caustics are bent. At 0°, the text is clear. At 90°, the text is completely blurred and distorted. This cycle repeats.
3. **Glints:** Occasional bright 'glints' of light catch the edges of the glass rings, adding a sense of material solidity.

## Technical Constraints
- Use high-quality ray-traced or screen-space refraction.
- The glass must have thickness and internal reflections, not just a flat image.
- The background water must be animated (ripples) to make the refraction dynamic.

## Acceptance Criteria
- The text reveal is purely optical (refraction), not a mask.
- The glass looks heavy and expensive.
- The motion is smooth and calming, not dizzying.
