# Basalt Column Cooling — Extended Brief

**Concept:**
Visualize the geological process of columnar jointing in basalt flows, but framed through an arid-minimalist lens. The focus is on the precision of nature's geometry and the silence of cooling earth. It is not a violent explosion, but a slow, tense release of thermal energy manifested as perfect, angular cracks.

**Palette:**
- **Core Stone:** #3a3a38 (Dark basalt, cooling)
- **Light Stone:** #d4cfc4 (Bone/dust-covered rock)
- **Highlight:** #f2eadd (Harsh desert sun/warm haze)
- **Shadow:** #22211f (Deep fissures)

**Typography & UI:**
- Minimal or no text. If present, use a thin, technical sans-serif (e.g., 'Inter' or 'Roboto Mono' variant) for temperature readouts, kept small and unobtrusive.
- Labels: "TEMP: 1100°C → 400°C", "STRUCTURE: HEXAGONAL".

**Layout:**
- **Desktop:** Full-screen viewport. The camera slowly zooms out to reveal the scale of the field of columns.
- **Mobile:** Centered focus on the cracking mechanism. Tap to trigger the next crack propagation phase.

**Motion Brief:**
1.  **Entrance:** Surface is dark, slightly glowing with residual heat (subtle orange/red hue fading to gray).
2.  **Ambient:** Heat haze distortion ripples above the surface. Dust particles drift slowly from right to left.
3.  **Interaction/Loop:** Cracks begin to form at random points, branching out to meet other cracks in hexagonal patterns. As cracks open, the shadow inside deepens. The material color shifts from dark, smooth black to rough, lighter gray as it cools.

**Constraints:**
- No violent shattering.
- No green/blue cool tones; keep it warm/arid.
- Shadows must be high-contrast and sharp.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- The transition from 'hot' to 'cold' is readable through color and shadow depth.
- The crack geometry feels organic but structurally sound (hexagonal tendency).
- Atmosphere feels dry and hot, not wet or cool.
