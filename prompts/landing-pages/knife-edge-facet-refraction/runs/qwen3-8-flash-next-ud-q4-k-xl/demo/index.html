01 — The Kiss

Where two faces meet, the metal forgets itself.

At the very apex, the steel is ground to a point finer than the wavelength of visible light. There is no surface left to see — only the moment a ray bends and leaves. What you perceive as the edge is light itself, arrested.

02 — The Test

Turn the blade. Read the reflection.

A dull edge scatters light into a grey smear. A keen edge throws a single, hard line of fire across the face. Sharpeners do not measure sharpness with a ruler; they watch for the glint. We build our grinds to answer that gaze.

03 — The Geometry

Convex, not flat. Curved, not straight.

A flat bevel bites and stops. A convex bevel rolls through the cut, keeping contact with the board for a fraction of a millimetre longer — and holding its polish far longer in return. The curve is invisible. The cut is unforgettable.

From billet to edge

Six steps, no shortcuts.

01

Forge

Three layers — a hardened SG2 core folded between two softer jackets — are joined under pressure and heat, giving the blade both edge retention and forgiveness.

1050 °C
02

Grind

The blank is drawn across a slow belt at a fixed angle. The convex bevel is shaped by hand, not by template, so each face curves toward the same invisible apex.

15°
03

Harden

The blade is brought to critical temperature and quenched in oil, locking the crystal structure into its hardest, most brittle state — before it is asked to be anything else.

1020 °C
04

Temper

Two cycles in a low oven trade a little hardness for toughness. The steel learns to flex without failing — the difference between a blade and a shard of glass.

210 °C ×2
05

Polish

Up to twenty-four grits, from coarse stone to mirror film. The kasumi haze is left deliberately — a fog that lets the reflection travel across the face.

24 grit
06

Strop

The final pass is drawn along leather charged with diamond paste, folding the microscopic burr away until the edge reflects a single, unbroken line.

0.5 µm
