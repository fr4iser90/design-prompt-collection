See the Impressions → Commission a Run

In letterpress, the type does not rest on the paper — it bites into it. Every letter is a small, deliberate wound in the fiber, and the ink that pools in that wound is what gives a printed page its weight.

We still set metal by hand, still mix our own inks, and still run each sheet through a century-old platen press one impression at a time. There is no undo. There is only the bite.

"A printed page should feel like something you could run a finger across — and feel the letters."

The ink room

Mixed by eye, pressed by feel.

We keep a working palette of house inks — the warm charcoal that reads as black without the harshness, the deep oxblood that has been our signature since the first run, and the paper white that lives in every blind emboss.

Bring a swatch, a photograph, a memory of a color. We will mix it by hand and press a test so you can feel it before you commit to the run.
