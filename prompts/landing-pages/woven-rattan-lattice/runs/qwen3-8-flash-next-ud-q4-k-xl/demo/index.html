Our philosophy

A woven surface is never solid. It is a thousand decisions about where light may pass and where it must rest.

We build furniture the way basketry taught us — patiently, by hand, one crossing at a time. The result is not a barrier but a filter: a place for the sun to slow down, to dapple, to warm a room without ever scorching it.

Our rattan is harvested, cured and split on the island where it grows, then carried to a workshop where six weavers share a single loom. Nothing is rushed. Nothing is hidden behind a finish.

The weave

Six hands, one loom, forty hours

From raw cane to finished frame, every crossing is deliberate. Here is the passage a piece takes before it reaches your room.

01

Harvest & cure

Rattan is cut at dawn, stripped and sun-cured for weeks until it turns supple and honeyed.

~3 weeks
02

Split & sort

Each pole is split into strands of graded thickness — the spine of the weave and its fine weft.

2 days
03

The crossing

Strands pass over and under in a fixed rhythm, tightening the lattice until it holds its own weight.

20 hours
04

Binding the edge

The border is wrapped and locked, giving the open weave a finished, tactile rim you can run a hand along.

6 hours
05

Light test

Each piece is held to the sun. If the dapple is wrong, it goes back to the loom — never shipped.

1 day
Materials

Fibre, honestly named

No laminates, no plastic cane. Only what grows, bends and ages gracefully.

Primary

Rattan

Calamus · Indonesia

Solid, flexible and springy — the backbone of every frame we build.

Weft

Seagrass

Coastal harvest · Vietnam

Matte and sturdy, it fills the seat and resists the wear of daily sitting.

Accent

Water Hyacinth

River reed · Thailand

Soft and pale, woven into the panels that catch and scatter light.

Finish

Teak Oil

Cold-pressed · 3 coats

Hand-rubbed to protect and warm the fibre without sealing it shut.
