01 / The Method

We design for the second face of metal.

Copper is never finished on installation day. Our practice treats the first decade as a co-author — specifying alloys, seams and exposure so the building arrives at its truest surface.

Oxidation stageBare Copper
01

Specifying the alloy

We begin with the metal itself — selecting sheet copper, its temper and thickness so that grain, weight and oxidation rate match the climate it will face for a century.

20 oz / sqAlloy C11000Hard temper
02

Standing-seam craft

Every panel is hand-formed and soldered to a concealed batten. The geometry is drawn to shed water and to let the patina run in honest, streaking lines rather than stains.

200mm seamsConcealed clipHand-soldered
03

Letting it darken

Within a year the surface dulls from mirror-bright to a warm, matte bronze. We protect adjacent stone and glazing during this run-off, then step back and let weather do the drawing.

0–3 yrsMatte bronzeRun-off control
04

The green arrival

Over a decade, carbonate and sulfate compounds form a stable verdigris — a self-healing skin that halts further corrosion. The building reaches its final, quiet colour.

12–25 yrsStable skinSelf-sealing
05

A roof that outlives us

A properly weathered copper roof needs no coating, no paint, no replacement — only rain. It is the rare material that grows more valuable, and more beautiful, with every passing decade.

120+ yrsRecyclableZero coating

02 / Aging

A surface, measured in decades.

The colour journey of a copper roof under a temperate, maritime climate — the same arc every building we make is drawn toward.

YEAR 0

Bare Copper

Mirror-bright, warm and reflective. The metal is still itself.

YEAR 1–3

Matte Bronze

Oxide dulls the shine into a deep, even bronze brown.

YEAR 5–8

Weathered Brown

Streaks and shading appear as rain begins its slow drawing.

YEAR 10–15

Greening

Verdigris creeps in along sheltered seams and lower pitches.

YEAR 20+

Verdigris

A soft, uniform green skin — the classic copper patina.

YEAR 50+

Stabilized

Corrosion halts. The surface is sealed, permanent, at rest.
