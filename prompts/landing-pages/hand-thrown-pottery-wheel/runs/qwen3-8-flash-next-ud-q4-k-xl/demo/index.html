01 — The Wheel

Every piece begins as a spinning question.

Throwing is a conversation between the potter's hands and the clay's own memory of water. Here's how a lump of earth becomes something you'll hold every morning.

1

Center

Wedged and pressed to the wheel head, the clay must find its balance point before it can rise.

2

Open

Thumbs press down and out, coaxing a floor and walls from solid earth.

3

Shape

Fingers travel up and down, pulling the curve into a bowl, a vase, a quiet cup.

4

Fire

Trimmed, bisque-dried, glazed, and sent into the wood kiln where it finds its final color.

The Collection

Shelves of things still drying.

Each piece is thrown one at a time, glazed in small batches, and signed on the foot. No two are ever quite the same.

Beginner friendly
Learn the craft

Sit down. Get your hands wet.

Our evening and weekend sessions run in groups of six or fewer, so there's a potter's wheel and an attentive teacher for every pair of hands. No experience required — just a willingness to get a little muddy.

You'll throw, trim, and glaze your own pieces, then collect them fired a few weeks later. Clay, tools, apron, and kiln time are all included.
