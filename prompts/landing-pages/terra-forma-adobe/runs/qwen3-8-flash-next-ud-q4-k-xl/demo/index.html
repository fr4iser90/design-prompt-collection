01 — Sourcing

Material Origin

01

Every wall begins as earth, water, and time — pressed into form and left to the desert sun until it holds its own weight.

We excavate clay from the same arroyos that have yielded adobe for eight centuries. The soil is screened, blended with sand and straw, then cast into forms and cured in place. Nothing is fired; nothing is shipped. The material travels no further than the plot it will stand on.

Because the brick is born of its site, each structure carries the exact colour of its ground — a pigment no factory can replicate.

02 — Engineering

Structural Integrity

02

Adobe is not a soft material. It is a mass system — thick walls that store the day's heat and release it through the cold desert night.

Hover the section to reveal the cross-section. Load paths descend through compressed earth to a continuous rubble footing; the bond beam at the top ties the walls against lateral force.

From Ground
to Ground

04
01

Excavate

02

Mix

Screened earth is blended with sand and straw to a hand-pressable consistency.

03

Cast

Bricks are formed in timber moulds and turned to breathe on the ground.

04

Cure

Twenty-eight days of desert sun dry the walls to structural strength.

05

Build

Walls rise course by course, tied by a bond beam and set on rubble footing.
